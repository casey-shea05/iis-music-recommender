# Project Report - Brief and Criteria

# Assignment 2: Project Report

Make your intelligent interactive system and show how someone interacts with it. Explain what you made, why you made those choices and how the work developed. Choose a scope that suits the complexity of your project.

These projects will help you prepare for the final exam. Make sure you can explain your choices, relate them to module concepts and reflect on what you learned.

You may develop your idea, approach or features as you learn more. Explain significant changes through your design rationale or development record.

Use relevant concepts and design guidelines from lectures in Weeks 1–4 when explaining your design choices and interaction. Name the concept or guideline and explain how it informed your decision rather than simply listing terms.

## Your report should cover

- **Abstract, up to 300 words**
    - Explain the problem, your approach and what you made.
    - You may reuse an earlier abstract, but revise it so that it accurately describes this project, what you have developed and the current scope.
    - Identify the current scope of the project.
- **Design rationale and project explanation, around 1,000 words; maximum 1,100 words**
    - Explain your main design choices, why they suit the problem and the evidence supporting them.
    - Draw on your Assignment 1 literature, feedback and development experience.
    - Use relevant concepts and design guidelines from Weeks 1–4 to explain how your choices respond to the problem.
    - You can use the table below or another clear format.
    - You may include choices for features you have not developed. Explain why you would include them and mark them as planned.
- **System diagram**
    - Show the components, connections and information or control flows, including AI behaviour.
    - Identify what is implemented, simulated or still planned.
    - Use boxes, arrows and short labels rather than sentences or paragraphs. For help, see [Lucidchart’s guide to system architecture diagrams](https://lucid.co/blog/how-to-draw-architectural-diagrams/). You can use any drawing tool.
- **Storyboards and illustrated interaction**
    - Show two or three examples of someone using your system, including their actions, the system’s responses and relevant feedback or control.
- **Repository and development evidence**
    - Link your public GitHub repository and README, and identify the submitted commit or release.
    - Point the reader to the development record in your README.
- **References**
    - List the sources cited and acknowledge reused material.

Organise the report as you wish, but make it clear and easy to follow. Screenshots, simple sketches and AI-generated images are acceptable ways to illustrate an interaction. Do not spend excessive time drawing.

## Using AI

You may use AI to help develop the system and prepare the report, diagrams, storyboards and README. Check the output, correct mistakes and make sure it represents your project accurately. You are responsible for the design choices, claims and references you submit. Briefly record how you used AI in the README, including what you checked or changed. If you did not use AI, say so.

## What counts as developed work?

You may show an implementation, prototype, simulation or a walkthrough of developed screens and states. Clearly label placeholders, including hardware you could not develop, and explain what they represent. Show the interaction and the reasoning behind the functionality. Visual polish and the number of features are not the priority.

Your report’s illustrations and explanations should allow a reader to follow what someone does and how the system responds. A list of planned features alone does not show developed work.

Planned features can appear in your rationale and diagrams, but label them clearly. We assess the reasoning behind these choices separately from the evidence of what you have developed or simulated.

## Repository and README

Keep your project material in a public GitHub repository wherever possible. The README should explain:

- **Project status:** What you made, what works or is simulated, and what remains unfinished.
- **Technology stack:** The technologies and AI tools or models you used, what each does and any choices still undecided.
- **Running or inspecting the work:** How to run, replay or inspect what you have, including dependencies and setup where needed.
- **Development record:** The significant changes you made, why you made them and what you checked. Link relevant commits where available. You may group changes by feature or stage of development; screenshots of the commit history are not required.
- **AI assistance:** If you used AI, name the tools and explain what you asked them to help with, how you used or changed their suggestions and how you checked the result. A few relevant examples are enough; a full prompt or chat log is not required.
- **Additional files:** Where to find any accompanying documents or ZIP files and how to use them.

We will use the development record and relevant commit messages to understand how you approached the project. The number of commits is not a measure of quality. You do not need to repeat the README in a separate report appendix.

Keep the README brief. Short headings, bullets and setup commands are enough; it should help someone inspect your work, not become a second report. Include the information needed to use what you have, without a diary of every change. State the README’s prose word count near the top; code and setup commands do not need to be counted. There is no separate README word limit.

Never commit API keys to GitHub, even in a private repository. If a key is needed, explain how a reviewer can supply their own without including your key.

## Deliverable

One project report containing links to your repository and README. Submit an accompanying ZIP only for material that cannot reasonably go in the repository, and explain it in the README. No video is required for this assignment.

Write around **1,000 words** for the main report, with a maximum of **1,100 words**, plus an abstract of up to **300 words**. The main report and abstract together must not exceed **1,400 words**. The main report includes the design rationale and written explanations of your system, examples and development decisions.

You may reuse an earlier abstract, but revise it so that it accurately describes this project, what you have developed and the current scope. Keep the abstract within 300 words.

Put the total report word count on the first page. You may also give the abstract word count separately. Include all text in the rationale table in the total report count. If the report significantly exceeds its limit, we will stop reading after the first **1,300 counted words.**

Figures, diagrams, storyboards and their short labels or captions do not count. References, repository links and README content are also outside the limit. Do not put paragraphs of explanation inside figures or storyboards to avoid the word limit. Around 1,000 words will usually be a few pages, depending on layout; page count is not assessed. Put written explanations of design choices in the rationale, where they count towards the limit.
