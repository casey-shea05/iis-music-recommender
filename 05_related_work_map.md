# Related-work map and gap sentence

## Themes (two or three; name each in four words)

### Theme A — User control over recommendations
- Gratzer (2025): qualitative study (n = 19) finding that satisfaction with music recommendation features depends on perceived controllability, and that control needs vary by situation
- Jin et al. (2019): three experiments showing that musical sophistication affects acceptance of user controls in a music recommender

### Theme B — Explaining recommendations to users
- Millecamp et al. (2020): user study (perception and gaze) showing that users with high musical sophistication and low openness benefit most from explanations in a music recommender interface

### Theme C — Control and explanation combined
- Abel & Goddard (2023): proposes a concert recommender in which users give ideal and negative ideal songs with reasons when setting their preferences, and receive explanations of recommendations (the abstract reports no evaluation)
- Tsai & Brusilovsky (2021): controlled study (N = 50) in a conference-attendee recommender (not music); sliders plus explanations gave the highest experience ratings, and perceived control was higher with sliders, though the control construct failed a validity check in their structural model


## Gap sentence

These sources establish that users' control needs vary by situation and personal characteristics (A), that explanations benefit some users more than others (B), and that control and explanation can be combined in one interface (C), but none tests whether letting users give a reason at the moment they reject a recommended music track, with or without an explanation of why it was picked, changes their sense of control; that is the gap this project addresses.
